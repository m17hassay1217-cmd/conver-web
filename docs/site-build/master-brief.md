# PART A — THE BUSINESS AND DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## A1. What Converdive is

**Converdive** is an AI workflow implementation and automation agency specifically targeting **UK commercial cleaning contractors**.

Its prospective customers are owners, founders, directors and commercial operations managers of companies that provide contract cleaning services to offices, commercial buildings, industrial facilities, multi-site customers and similar organizations.

Converdive is NOT a cleaning company.

It does NOT sell cleaning appointments to homeowners, facilities managers or consumers.

It helps cleaning businesses investigate, design and potentially improve their existing **enquiry-to-confirmed-work commercial processes** using appropriately engineered AI, conventional automation, connected software and human oversight.

Relevant business activities include:

- Receiving and interpreting commercial enquiries
- Qualification and commercial feasibility assessment
- Gathering site-specific requirements
- Survey coordination
- Cleaning specifications and scope reconciliation
- Quotation preparation and revision
- Customer questions and approvals
- Commercial follow-ups
- Confirmed-job or contract handoff
- Identifying additional opportunities within existing customer accounts

These are potential intervention areas, not a claim that every cleaning contractor experiences the same problems.

The agency's approach is strategy-first, workflow-specific and implementation-oriented.

## A2. The story this website must tell

The website is a progressive narrative.

Every section answers a question raised by the previous section while creating sufficient curiosity to continue.

The narrative sequence is:

| Section | Core question |
|---|---|
| 01 — Hero | How well does your enquiry-to-booking process work? |
| 02 — Industry choice | Why UK commercial cleaning companies specifically? |
| 03 — Problems | What consequential problems could occur? |

| 09 — Value | What could these improvements be worth? |
| 10 — Contact | How do we begin a conversation? |

Do not prematurely reveal information reserved for later sections.

For example, Section 01 should not show the full commercial workflow, and Section 03 should not reveal all the solutions.

5. Maintain strong typography and clear reading order even during motion.
6. Use restrained hover effects and more substantial scroll-driven transitions where warranted.
7. Visually distinguish hypothetical improvements from demonstrated achievements.
8. Do not invent testimonials, client logos, business statistics, conversion results or performance claims.
9. Respect existing contractor processes and human commercial authority.
10. Use creative composition, not generic startup templates.

# SECTION 04 — ROOT-CAUSE CROSS-SECTIONS
## Theme: Looking beneath the visible problem

### 1. Purpose

Show that the visible problem may be the downstream consequence of multiple intermediate conditions.

Do not claim the illustrated causal chains are proven universal root causes.

Use exactly four causal examples, each with a maximum of TWO intermediate causal layers between the visible problem and the potential controllable upstream condition.

### 2. The four causal chains

#### CAUSAL CHAIN 01 — Qualification

**Visible problem:** Valuable sales capacity spent on unsuitable enquiries.

↓ **Mediator 1:** Enquiries follow inappropriate assessment or survey routes.

↓ **Mediator 2:** Scope, complexity, location and feasibility are not evaluated together.

↓ **Potential controllable root:** No dependable context-sensitive qualification and routing mechanism.

#### CAUSAL CHAIN 02 — Specification integrity

**Visible problem:** Cleaning proposals repeatedly revised or incorrectly scoped.

↓ **Mediator 1:** Proposed scope does not fully reflect the latest agreed requirements.

↓ **Mediator 2:** Survey observations, customer messages and subsequent changes are not reconciled reliably.

↓ **Potential controllable root:** No traceable, version-controlled requirements-to-proposal process.

#### CAUSAL CHAIN 03 — Proposal progression

**Visible problem:** Interested prospects remain undecided after proposals.

↓ **Mediator 1:** Customer questions, revisions or approval dependencies remain unresolved.

↓ **Mediator 2:** The necessary next decision, responsible person or suitable timing is unclear.

↓ **Potential controllable root:** No dependable mechanism tracking decision readiness and next actions.

#### CAUSAL CHAIN 04 — Account expansion

**Visible problem:** Additional recurring-contract opportunities go unidentified.

↓ **Mediator 1:** New requirements are treated as isolated requests.

↓ **Mediator 2:** Their relationship to existing customer sites and agreements goes unrecognized.

↓ **Potential controllable root:** No dependable connection between customer accounts, site-specific requirements and new commercial opportunities.

### 3. First transition: surface to cross-section

The first stained surface from Section 03 rotates approximately 90 degrees around its appropriate vertical local axis.

Use genuine 3D yaw to expose its thickness, not a flat screen-plane rotation masquerading as depth.

As the thickness becomes visible, the surface expands into a large layered cross-section.

Separate its internal layers slightly.

Reveal all four causal levels simultaneously in a single view.

Each level should have a clear, short annotation and a fine connective path to the next.

The entire chain should be comprehensible without the viewer having to scroll through each causal unit separately.

### 4. Main section composition

Implement a **scroll-controlled, depth-scaled horizontal coverflow carousel**.

The active causal surface occupies the center and appears closest to the viewer.

Adjacent surfaces remain smaller, slightly translucent and positioned behind it in perspective.

As the visitor scrolls:

- current surface reduces in scale;
- it moves sideways and recedes along the depth axis;
- the next surface advances from behind;
- the next surface scales up and settles at the center;
- its four-level causal cross-section becomes readable.

Progress through four surfaces in sequence.

Avoid spinning the entire composition excessively.

Use smooth transform interpolation, controlled perspective, stable camera position and consistent lighting.

### 5. Scroll behavior

Pin the primary section temporarily during horizontal progression.

Scrolling should advance through the four scenes without excessive dead distance.

Preserve reversibility when scrolling upward.

Allow keyboard and touch navigation.

### 6. Exit: all four root causes connect

Once the final surface has been examined:

- the active full-size surface rapidly scales down;
- all four surfaces assemble into a compact horizontal arrangement near the top of the viewport;
- their respective root-cause marker locations remain identifiable;
- a thin luminous connection path travels from RIGHT TO LEFT through those four upstream markers.

The path then performs a smooth U-turn near the left side and descends toward Section 05.

# SECTION 09 — BUSINESS VALUE CALCULATOR
## Theme: Quantifying possible value

### 1. Opening question

**“What could a better enquiry-to-booking process be worth?”**

“Explore potential value using your own figures and transparent assumptions.”

### 2. Visual character

**Right:** live calculated results.

### 3. Calculator inputs

Build an actually functional calculator with:

1. Monthly commercial enquiries
2. Current enquiry-to-booking rate
3. Hypothetical booking-rate improvement in percentage points
4. Average value of a newly booked job or contract
5. Value type: one-off job value or estimated first-year contract value

Optional efficiency inputs:

6. Administrative hours potentially saved per week
7. Average relevant hourly employment cost

Provide reasonable editable example values, clearly labeled as **illustrative assumptions**, not industry statistics.

Use validated numeric fields and sliders where appropriate.

### 4. Calculation logic

Let:

- `N` = monthly enquiries
- `b` = current booking rate as a decimal
- `d` = assumed improvement in percentage points converted to a decimal
- `V` = average selected booking/contract value

Current expected monthly bookings:

`N × b`

Illustrative additional monthly bookings:

`N × min(d, 1 − b)`

Illustrative value of additional work booked in one month:

`Additional bookings × V`

Annualized new-work booking-value scenario:

`Monthly additional booking value × 12`

Label these values carefully.

If the visitor selects **first-year contract value**, explain that the output estimates the face value of additional contracts potentially booked, not revenue necessarily earned or recognized that month.

Do not label gross contract value as profit.

For administrative efficiency:

Monthly hours saved:

`Weekly hours saved × 52 / 12`

Illustrative monthly staff-cost equivalent:

`Monthly hours saved × Hourly cost`

Keep staff-cost equivalents separate from revenue opportunities to avoid misleading double-counting.

Do not claim net ROI unless implementation cost, ongoing cost and relevant profit assumptions have been supplied.

### 5. Result presentation

Three result areas:

**Recovered Opportunity**

Potential additional bookings per month.

**Value of Additional Work**

Illustrative monthly new-work booking value and annualized equivalent.

**Efficiency Potential**

Possible administrative hours and staff-cost equivalent saved.

Make the central value legible and prominent.

Use restrained animated numeric transitions when inputs change.

### 6. Transparency

Always display:

“These estimates are illustrative, not guaranteed outcomes. Actual results depend on enquiry quality, current processes, implementation scope and commercial circumstances.”

Provide a visible explanation of the assumptions.

The calculator must function without requiring an account.

### 7. Exit

Transition to the final contact area using ordinary, clean vertical scrolling.
